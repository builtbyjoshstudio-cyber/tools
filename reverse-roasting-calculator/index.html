How to plan your cooking timeline backward

Most cooking guidance tells you how long something takes — but the question that actually matters at dinnertime is the reverse: what time do I need to start so it's ready when I want to eat? This tool works backward from your target serve time. You enter the meat, its weight, and when you want to sit down, and it builds a chronological schedule — when to preheat, when the meat goes in, when to pull it, and how long it rests — so everything lands on time.

The timeline accounts for the parts people forget: oven preheat, the temperature drop and recovery when cold meat goes in, and the resting window after it comes out, when the meat keeps cooking and the juices redistribute. Larger cuts need a longer runway, and skipping the rest is the most common way a well-cooked roast still ends up dry on the plate. Always confirm doneness with a calibrated thermometer and follow USDA minimum safe temperatures for poultry, pork, and ground meat.

How the calculator handles a pork roast

The calculator has two pork profiles. Pork Loin Roast follows the USDA roasting chart: about 20 minutes per pound in a 350°F (175°C) oven, so a 3-pound loin is about an hour. Pork Shoulder & Hams runs lower and slower: about 40 minutes per pound in a 250°F (120°C) oven. The rest is set by weight either way: 10 minutes under 2 pounds, 20 minutes up to 8 pounds, and 40 minutes above that. Choose the cut, enter the weight and the time you want to eat, and it works backward — when to preheat, when the roast goes in, when to pull it, and the rest before you serve or shred it. A 6-pound pork shoulder runs about four hours in the oven plus the rest, so a 6 p.m. dinner means starting early in the afternoon.

The slow shoulder schedule is what gives a tough, fatty cut time to turn tender. A lean loin is the opposite: it only needs to reach temperature, and overshooting dries it out. The calculator schedules the cook from the weight; it doesn’t read the meat for you, so confirm doneness with a thermometer and follow USDA guidance: a 145°F safe minimum for whole pork cuts, with a 3-minute rest. For the exact temperature to pull at for your cut and doneness, use the companion Perfect Roast Pull Temp Calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a reverse cooking timeline?

Instead of working forward from when you start, you work backward from when you want to eat. You set your target dinner time and the tool schedules each step — prep, roasting, and resting — so everything finishes together.

How do I know when to start cooking?

Enter your protein, its weight, and your target serving time. The tool calculates total cook time from the weight, adds resting and prep buffers, and tells you the exact time to begin.

How long should meat rest after roasting?

It depends on size — small cuts need only a few minutes, large roasts benefit from 20–40 minutes. Resting lets juices redistribute and accounts for carryover cooking.

Are these cooking times exact?

They're planning estimates. Oven calibration, starting meat temperature, and bone-in versus boneless all affect real cook time. Always confirm doneness with a calibrated thermometer and follow USDA safe internal temperatures.

How long do you cook a pork loin roast per pound?

About 20 minutes per pound in a 350°F (177°C) oven, per the USDA roasting chart, for a 2 to 5 pound bone-in or boneless loin roast. A 3-pound loin takes about an hour. Cook it to a minimum internal temperature of 145°F (63°C) and rest it at least 3 minutes.

How long does a pork shoulder take in the oven?

The USDA chart lists a 3 to 6 pound Boston butt at 45 minutes per pound in a 350°F (177°C) oven. This calculator's Pork Shoulder & Hams profile schedules a lower, slower cook: about 40 minutes per pound at 250°F (120°C). Pulled pork is cooked well past the safe minimum, until it is fork tender, so leave extra time and let the thermometer and the fork decide.

What temperature should a pork roast be cooked to?

Whole pork cuts such as loin, shoulder, and fresh ham are safe at a minimum internal temperature of 145°F (63°C) followed by a rest of at least 3 minutes. Ground pork needs 160°F (71°C).
