How to plan your cooking timeline backward

Most cooking guidance tells you how long something takes — but the question that actually matters at dinnertime is the reverse: what time do I need to start so it's ready when I want to eat? This tool works backward from your target serve time. You enter the meat, its weight, and when you want to sit down, and it builds a chronological schedule — when to preheat, when the meat goes in, when to pull it, and how long it rests — so everything lands on time.

The timeline accounts for the parts people forget: oven preheat, the temperature drop and recovery when cold meat goes in, and the resting window after it comes out, when the meat keeps cooking and the juices redistribute. Larger cuts need a longer runway, and skipping the rest is the most common way a well-cooked roast still ends up dry on the plate. Always confirm doneness with a calibrated thermometer and follow USDA minimum safe temperatures for poultry, pork, and ground meat.

How the calculator handles a pork roast

The calculator has three pork profiles, all taken from the USDA roasting chart. Pork Loin Roast: about 20 minutes per pound in a 350°F (175°C) oven, so a 3-pound loin is about an hour. Pork Shoulder (Boston Butt): about 45 minutes per pound at 350°F (175°C). Ham: about 20 minutes per pound at 325°F (165°C), the middle of the USDA range for whole and half hams; spiral-cut and boneless hams run faster. The loin and ham rest by weight: 10 minutes under 2 pounds, 20 minutes up to 8 pounds, and 40 minutes above that. A shoulder is held for 1 hour in an insulated cooler, the minimum the Perfect Roast Pull Temp Calculator calls for on barbecue cuts. Choose the cut, enter the weight and the time you want to eat, and it works backward — when to preheat, when the roast goes in, when to pull it, and the rest before you serve or shred it. A 6-pound pork shoulder runs about four and a half hours in the oven plus the 1-hour hold, so a 6 p.m. dinner means the oven goes on at noon.

A shoulder is a tough, fatty cut, and the long schedule is what gives it time to turn tender. For pull-apart pork, keep going past the schedule until a fork twists easily. A lean loin is the opposite: it only needs to reach temperature, and overshooting dries it out. The calculator schedules the cook from the weight; it doesn’t read the meat for you, so confirm doneness with a thermometer and follow USDA guidance: a 145°F safe minimum for whole pork cuts, with a 3-minute rest. For the exact temperature to pull at for your cut and doneness, use the companion Perfect Roast Pull Temp Calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a reverse cooking timeline?

Instead of working forward from when you start, you work backward from when you want to eat. You set your target dinner time and the tool schedules each step — prep, roasting, and resting — so everything finishes together.

How do I know when to start cooking?

Enter your protein, its weight, and your target serving time. The tool calculates total cook time from the weight, adds resting and prep buffers, and tells you the exact time to begin.

How long should meat rest after roasting?

It depends on size — small cuts need only a few minutes, large roasts benefit from 20–40 minutes. Barbecue cuts hold longer: the timeline keeps a pork shoulder in an insulated cooler for 1 hour and a brisket for 2 hours before slicing. Resting lets juices redistribute and accounts for carryover cooking.

Are these cooking times exact?

They're planning estimates. Oven calibration, starting meat temperature, and bone-in versus boneless all affect real cook time. Always confirm doneness with a calibrated thermometer and follow USDA safe internal temperatures.

How long do you cook a pork loin roast per pound?

About 20 minutes per pound in a 350°F (177°C) oven, per the USDA roasting chart, for a 2 to 5 pound bone-in or boneless loin roast. A 3-pound loin takes about an hour. Cook it to a minimum internal temperature of 145°F (63°C) and rest it at least 3 minutes.

How long does a pork shoulder take in the oven?

About 45 minutes per pound in a 350°F (177°C) oven, per the USDA roasting chart, for a 3 to 6 pound Boston butt, so a 6-pound shoulder takes about four and a half hours. That is the profile this calculator uses. Pulled pork is cooked well past the safe minimum, until it is fork tender, so leave extra time and let the thermometer and the fork decide.

What temperature should a pork roast be cooked to?

Whole pork cuts such as loin, shoulder, and fresh ham are safe at a minimum internal temperature of 145°F (63°C) followed by a rest of at least 3 minutes. Ground pork needs 160°F (71°C).

How long do you cook a ham per pound?

In a 325°F (163°C) oven, the USDA chart gives 15 to 18 minutes per pound for a fully cooked whole bone-in ham and 18 to 24 for a half, or 18 to 20 and 22 to 25 for a ham labelled cook before eating. This calculator plans on 20 minutes per pound. Reheat a fully cooked ham to 140°F (60°C), and cook a cook-before-eating or fresh ham to 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest.
