How pan conversion is calculated

Swapping a recipe from one pan to another comes down to a single number: how much bigger or smaller the new pan is. This calculator works that out from the volume of each pan — its footprint times its depth. A round pan's footprint is π times the radius squared, a square, rectangular or loaf pan is just length times width, and that is multiplied by the depth you enter. Muffin tins and Bundt pans have no sizes to enter, so they use their brim-full capacity: 6 cups for a standard 12-cup muffin tin (each cup holds about half a cup) and 10 cups for the 10-cup Bundt. Footprint times depth runs about 13% over what a real pan holds, because of sloped sides and rounded corners, so those two capacities are raised by the same 13% to compare like with like. Divide the target pan's volume by the original's and you get the recipe multiplier: a value above 1 means you'll need more batter to fill the new pan, below 1 means less.

Scaling by volume fills the new pan to the same level as the original. When both pans are the same depth, as standard 8x8 and 9x13 pans are at 2 inches, that's the same as matching their footprints. Loaf pans are 2.5 to 3 inches deep, so a 9x5 loaf recipe scales to about 1.7x for a 9x13, not the 2.6x its footprint suggests. The place to be careful is depth: move a recipe from a shallow pan into a much deeper one and the batter sits much taller, which makes the outside set long before the center cooks through. When the calculator detects that kind of swap it flags it, and the fix is to lower the oven temperature by about 25°F and add 15 to 20 minutes, checking the center with a toothpick before pulling. Whenever the pan size changes meaningfully, treat the original bake time as a starting point and watch the pan, not the clock.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert a recipe to a different pan size?

Compare the volume of the two pans (footprint times depth) — the ratio tells you how to scale the batter. A pan that holds twice as much needs roughly twice the batter. This tool calculates the exact multiplier for you.

Why does pan depth matter?

Batter depth drives bake time and texture. Pans of the same depth, like standard 2-inch 8x8 and 9x13 pans, can be matched by area alone, but a loaf pan is 2.5 to 3 inches deep: scaling a loaf recipe by area keeps that deep batter and can fill a 9x13 to the rim. Scaling by volume fills the new pan to the same level instead. When the new pan is deeper, the batter sits deeper and needs a longer bake, which the tool flags.

What pan shapes can I convert between?

Round, square, rectangular, loaf, Bundt, and muffin pans.

Will the baking time change when I swap pans?

Often, yes. A shallower pan bakes faster, a deeper one slower. The tool flags when a swap pushes batter into a notably deeper or shallower setup so you know to check doneness early or extend the time.
